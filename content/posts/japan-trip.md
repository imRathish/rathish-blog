### A quick look at the geography that we explored

**Before You Dive into the Itinerary: A Few Things to Keep in Mind**

1.  **Transit Will Eat Your Budget 🍥**  
    A big chunk of your expenses will go into transit — trains, buses, and other travel. Japan’s transportation isn’t cheap, but it’s incredibly efficient and gets you everywhere you need to be. Plan accordingly!
2.  **The Railway System is a Puzzle (At First) 🚉**  
    The first two days might feel like a crash course in Japanese transportation. Deciphering signs, figuring out train routes, and understanding the different passes (trust me, passes are a lifesaver for saving money) can be overwhelming, but you’ll get the hang of it in no time. Most signboards are in English, so you’ll find your way easily.
3.  **Be Ready to Walk… A Lot 👟**  
    Japan is a paradise for walkers, and you’ll definitely rack up those steps exploring cities, temples, shrines, and even train stations (some of them feel like small towns!). Comfortable shoes are an absolute must, and trust me, your feet will thank you by the end of each day.
4.  **Google Translate is Your Best Friend 📱**  
    Whether it’s reading menus, signs, or even having quick conversations with locals, **Google Translate** will be your ultimate travel buddy. Its camera feature is a game-changer for translating text instantly. Download the Japanese language pack in advance so you can use it offline too.
5.  **Google Maps for the Win 🗺️**  
    Navigating Japan’s extensive transit system can be tricky, but **Google Maps** makes it a breeze. It’s incredibly detailed and shows you everything from train schedules to platform numbers and even walking routes. Save your destinations offline just in case you lose internet access during the day.
6.  **Early Sunsets and Closing Times 🌅**  
    By 4:30 PM, the sun starts setting, and it’s pitch dark soon after. This means your days start early! Make sure to check the closing times of the places you plan to visit the next day and set your alarms accordingly. Japan rewards the early risers! ⏰

### **Day 0: The Journey Begins — Chennai 🛫 Delhi 🛫 Tokyo (Narita Airport)**

**Packing and Prepping for Japan: Tips and Essentials**

Let’s start with a big one: **pack light!** Seriously, lugging around multiple suitcases on Japan’s trains is a challenge you don’t want. A single medium or large trolley with enough clothes for 14 days is more than enough. Here’s a fun hack: pack about 70% of what you’ll need and buy the remaining 30% in Japan. This forces you to shop (yay!), lets you enjoy wearing stylish Japanese clothes while you’re there, and gives you built-in luggage space for your cool new purchases! 🎒👘

**Checklist Tip:**  
Before you go, create a good old-fashioned checklist in your favourite phone app and cross-check it before you leave. Trust me, this saves so much stress later!

**Must-Have Items to Buy in Advance:**

1.  **Universal Adapter 🔌**  
    Japanese sockets are different, so you’ll need a universal adapter. I got [this one](https://www.amazon.in/dp/B0CN38CGRJ?ref=ppx_yo2ov_dt_b_fed_asin_title&th=1), and it was totally worth it. It let me charge my iPhone, iWatch, and camera batteries all at once! Pick whichever works best for you, but don’t skip this — charging struggles aren’t fun.
2.  **Forex Card 💳**  
    I used the **Niyo Forex Card**, and it worked like a dream. You simply transfer INR to your Niyo account, and the card converts it to yen seamlessly for use in Japan. Do some research and choose the forex card that suits you best, but having one is a game-changer.
3.  **Internet Access 📶**  
    Wondering how you’ll stay connected in Japan? Your Airtel or Jio probably won’t work there unless you splurge on an international plan. If your phone supports eSIMs, the best option is to get an **eSIM with a local data plan** — it’s way cheaper! I used **Ubigi**, and it was super convenient. Just download the app, sign up in advance, and activate your data plan a few hours before leaving India. Simple, affordable, and reliable!   
    _No eSIM? No worries!_ I’ll cover options for those without eSIM support on **Day 1** of the itinerary.
4.  **Klook Passes/Tickets 🎟️**  
    Japan has countless attractions, and popular spots can get fully booked quickly. Using platforms like **Klook** to book tickets or passes in advance is a lifesaver. They often come with discounts and skip-the-line perks, making your trip smoother and more budget-friendly. Ideally, book these tickets **1–2 months in advance** to secure your spot, especially for high-demand attractions.

### **Day 1:** Touchdown in Narita ✈️ and Heading Straight to Kyoto

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*VA1SH8Bf0VzzlZ5dH2sXVg.jpeg)

Narita airport

We touched down at Narita, breezed through immigration, and headed straight to the restroom. First things first: brushed our teeth, freshened up, and yes, we used one of Japan’s _famous_ automatic toilets. Seriously, those things are a work of genius — easily one of the best inventions ever! 😄 🚽✨

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*CBNT0CyyPw7AtHNHM4yKxw.png)

Japan Toilet

If you already have an eSIM with a paid data plan, your internet should start working as soon as you touch down — smooth and seamless. But if your device doesn’t support eSIM, don’t worry! You can buy a **physical SIM** at the airport, although it’s a bit pricier than the eSIM option.

Next, you’ll need some cash. Head to an ATM inside the airport and withdraw **20,000 yen**, which should be enough to cover your expenses for getting to Kyoto.

> **Narita → Shinagawa (Tokyo) → Kyoto**

Narita Airport is quite a distance from Tokyo, so here’s what you need to do:

1.  **Head Down to Narita Railway Station:**  
    Use the escalators to reach the train station inside the airport.
2.  **Buy Tickets for the Narita Express (N’EX):**  
    This is the fastest and most convenient way to get to Tokyo. This might cost around (~1000 yen)
3.  **Get an IC Card (SUICA/ICOCA):**  
    At the ticket vending machines, buy a **SUICA card**, or if you’re planning to wait until Kyoto, you can get an **ICOCA card** there. This might need a deposit of around 300–500 yen

> These IC cards are rechargeable and incredibly useful — they work almost everywhere, from trains and buses to convenience stores and vending machines. Think of them as Japan’s alternative to a debit card. Trust me, getting one is a no-brainer and will make your travels so much easier! All **IC cards** in Japan work the same way — they just have different names depending on the region. Whether it’s a **SUICA**, **ICOCA**, or any other, they’re all interchangeable and can be used across the country for trains, buses, and even in convenience stores. So don’t worry too much about which one you get — getting _any one_ will do the job perfectly! 💳✨

Now hop aboard the **beautiful Narita Express** and enjoy the scenic 1-hour journey to **Shinagawa Station (Tokyo)**.

From here, it’s time to experience one of Japan’s most iconic marvels: the **Shinkansen**, aka the bullet train! 🚄 This ride isn’t just about speed; it’s about _flying through stunning landscapes_, including a breathtaking view of **Mount Fuji** (on clear days) as you travel from Shinagawa (Tokyo) to Kyoto.

Head to the **JR ticket counter** and grab your Shinkansen tickets. With a reservation, it’ll cost around **7,000 yen** — worth every penny for this unforgettable experience.

For the next **2 hours**, sit back and enjoy the ride on one of the world’s fastest trains, cruising at an average speed of **220 km/h**. It’s smooth, quiet, and downright exhilarating! 🚅✨

After arriving at **Kyoto Station**, the first thing to do is pull out your map (or Google Maps) and find the directions to your hotel. We stayed at [**Room&Bed Fun Stay Kyoto**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/RuxEcYJEN8ScNA8R6), and trust me, the walk to your hotel is more than just a commute — it’s an experience.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*aTygpeSun6GVHSPLgc6vkg.jpeg)

As you stroll through the streets of Kyoto, you’ll probably find yourself lost in thought, comparing the little things between Japan and India. From the spotless sidewalks to the small yet forward-thinking details in daily life, it’s hard not to admire how far ahead they are in even the simplest things.

Before you know it, you’ll reach your hotel. Check-in, freshen up, and then it’s time to decide where you want to eat. Take a leisurely evening walk through the calming streets of Japan and soak in the serene night vibes. Finally, indulge in some delicious Japanese food — you’ve earned it after a long day of travel! 🍣🌙✨

### **Day 2: Morning at Kinkakuji ✨, Afternoon at Toei Kyoto Studio 🎥**

Our go-to breakfast spot in Kyoto was **Shinshindo — Teramachi**. A little pricier than a typical Chennai breakfast, sure — but the ambiance, presentation, and quality of food made it _so_ worth it. My usual combo? A comforting pork dish with a hot cup of coffee. Bonus: it was super close to our stay, which worked out well because, let’s be honest, we were always running late in the mornings 😅.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*2vXwo5d7SyhXQIz8pwhFLg.jpeg)

After breakfast, we hopped on a bus from the nearby stand and headed to **Kinkakuji — The Golden Pavilion**. The place was bustling with visitors, especially school kids in uniform — definitely looked like a school trip day. The weather was sunny and the golden temple? Absolutely dazzling under that bright sunlight. Autumn was just beginning to show, with hints of red and yellow creeping onto the leaves.

![kinkakuji](/images/japan/kinkakuji.jpg)

It was a clean, serene, and picturesque place — but the sun was intense, and we started to feel the travel fatigue creeping in. Thankfully, a quick water break and some ice creams brought us back to life.

From there, we caught a bus to **Kitano Hakubai-Cho Station**, which felt like a quiet dead-end station at a crossroads. From here, we boarded the **Randen tram line**, a charming little purple single-coach tram. We got off at **Satsueisho-mae Station** and walked about 1 km to reach the **Toei Kyoto Studio Park**.

This park is a gem — it’s a theme park-meets-film set that immerses you in the world of Japanese period dramas. The sets are so authentic that many are still used for real filming! You’ll find cool attractions, live shows, and even the chance to dress up like you’re in a samurai film.

We even bumped into two performers dressed as samurai. One of them was super friendly — handed over his fake sword, showed us how to pose, and we attempted some epic photos (key word: _attempted_ 😅). We caught a samurai action show and explored a brand-new set with green screens. We even filmed a goofy little 2-minute scene with our friends — felt like mini movie stars for a moment! 🎬

One floor up was something straight out of our childhood: a **Power Rangers exhibit**! Seeing all the red rangers in one frame was pure nostalgia. 🥹

![toei-studio](/images/japan/toei-studio.jpg)

After soaking in the studio vibes, we walked 15 minutes to **Hanazono Station** and took the train back to **Kyoto Station**. And wow — Kyoto Station itself is so massive and futuristic that it looks more like an airport or a luxury shopping mall.

Outside, we got a perfect view of the **Kyoto Tower**, glowing in red, white, and blue. It literally looked like a giant _kuthu vilakku_ (traditional lamp) lighting up the night sky. 🏮

Right opposite was **Aeon Mall**, calling out to us like a siren. Of course, we gave in and did a bit of clothes and shoe shopping — because why not? Retail therapy is a valid tourist activity. 😄🛍️

![alt text](/images/japan/kyoto-station.jpg)

Finally, we headed back to our stay, feet aching but hearts full. Slept like babies after a day well spent.

### **Day 3: A Day Trip to Nara — Deer, Temples, and Serenity 🦌✨**

The day began just like the last — with a hearty breakfast at **Shinshindo (Teramachi)**. By now, it was officially our morning ritual. But unlike the sunny Day 2, this one came with a light drizzle. And honestly? Japan’s clean streets looked even more aesthetic under a grey sky and wet pavement — like a live-action Ghibli scene.

We caught a train from **Kyoto to Nara Station**. The weather was breezy and overcast — perfect for slow wandering. As soon as we arrived, we treated ourselves to some **delicious ice cream** right at the entrance of Nara Park. Because why not have ice cream in the rain? 😋

**Nara** is known not just for its temples and shrines, but also for its **adorable wild deer** that roam freely in Nara Park. The moment we crossed the first big torii gate, we were greeted by a herd of deer chilling like they owned the place (which, to be fair, they kinda do).

![alt text](/images/japan/nara.jpg)

We made our way to the grand **Tōdaiji Temple**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Japan’s most iconic landmarks. It’s home to the **Daibutsu**, a massive bronze statue of Buddha Vairocana, housed in what is claimed to be the **largest wooden structure in the world**.

And yes — the Great Buddha truly _was_ great. So great, in fact, that my **Nikon D5600 with a 35mm lens** couldn’t capture it up close. I had to keep backing up just to fit the whole thing in the frame. Could I have just used my phone? Of course! But guess what — my phone died _just_ before I entered. Divine intervention? Maybe a sign to soak it all in without distractions 😅.

The temple interior was serene and beautiful. There were multiple idols, intricate wooden carvings, and even a **miniature model of the entire temple**. One of the coolest finds was the **incense altar**, which filled the hall with a calming fragrance.

![alt text](/images/japan/todaiji.jpg)

This is also where we bought our **first stamp book (Goshuincho)** — and here’s a pro tip: **buy one early in your trip**. Almost every major shrine, temple, and even train station in Japan has a unique stamp. Collecting them becomes a fun ritual, and flipping through them back home will bring back all the nostalgia in one go. Trust me — it’s the best kind of souvenir. 🧾

### **Day 4: A Rainy Day with Castles, Kobe Beef & Neon Nights — Himeji & Osaka Edition 🌧️🏯🍖🌆**

We kicked off the day just like the others — our now go-to breakfast spot, **Shinshindo — Teramachi**, fueled us up for the long trip ahead.

From **Kyoto**, we took a **2-hour train ride to Himeji**, and while it was long, the **rain-dotted window views** made it absolutely worth it. Somewhere along the way, we caught sight of the **Akashi Kaikyo Bridge** — a jaw-dropping suspension bridge connecting Kobe (on Honshu) to Awaji Island. This engineering marvel held the record for the **world’s longest central span** (1,991m!) until Turkey’s 1915 Çanakkale Bridge took the crown.

And yep, **Kobe** — if that name rings a bell, you’re right. It’s the land of the legendary **Kobe beef**. 😋  
 The bridge, silhouetted under overcast skies, was picture-perfect — majestic and moody.

We reached **Himeji Station** and walked for about 30 minutes to reach the grand **Himeji Castle**. The castle sits at the end of a wide, straight road, and as you approach, it slowly reveals itself through the mist — just like a scene from a samurai film.

At the entrance, a scenic little bridge leads you to the ticket counter. And here’s something unexpected but super cool: the guide pamphlets were available in **Tamil**! Clearly, Himeji sees its fair share of desi travelers! 🇮🇳

**Himeji-jō**, or the **White Heron Castle**, is the finest surviving example of **17th-century Japanese castle architecture**. It’s a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**, a **Japanese National Treasure**, and one of the country’s **Three Famous Castles**. The stark white walls and graceful design truly live up to its name.

![alt text](/images/japan/himeji.jpg)

We climbed all the way to the top, and the view from there was absolutely magical. **Raindrops trickled off the curved rooftops**, framing the roads below in a poetic, almost cinematic way.

Once we descended, it was time to fulfill a major food goal — we tried the **famous Kobe beef**. And wow, it absolutely lived up to the hype. The texture, the flavor — it pretty much melted in the mouth. Pure bliss. 🥩

After that, we roamed around Himeji’s shopping streets, grabbed a few **fridge magnets** and souvenirs. The **long, narrow lanes** reminded me of South Indian temple corridors — perfectly aligned and seemingly never-ending. You get tired just looking at them 😅.

![alt text](/images/japan/kobe-beef.jpg)

But we weren’t done yet! On our way back to Kyoto, we made a **pitstop at Osaka** to explore the city at night. And Osaka at night? It’s a **vibe**.

The streets were alive with **neon signs**, **glowing red lanterns**, and **people strolling under transparent umbrellas** — like a scene straight out of an anime. We grabbed a hot coffee from **Starbucks**, which was perfect for the drizzly weather.

As we stepped outside, the sight of dozens of clear umbrellas glowing under city lights was honestly so beautiful, it stayed with us. We even stumbled upon a **3D billboard featuring a cat** — such a random yet amazing little surprise.



With our hearts full and feet tired, we made our way back to **Kyoto** and wrapped up the night with some well-earned sleep in the cool, cozy air.

This was also our **final night in Kyoto**, and in this stay, we had built a bit of a routine — morning breakfasts at Shinshindo, evening strolls, getting lost and found in train stations, and crashing into bed after days filled with walking, wonder, and wagyu. Kyoto felt like a quiet, graceful friend — one that left us with peaceful memories and way too many photos. 📸🌙

### **Day 5: The Great Buddha 🕉️ and the Stunning Amaharashi Coast 🌊**

We checked out of our cozy Kyoto stay and had one final breakfast at **Shinshindo** — bittersweet, but delicious as always. It was time to **head north**, and with that, time to throw on an extra layer. Our destination? **Toyama**.

We boarded a train and arrived at **Hotel Livemax Toyama**, our stop for just one night. Now here’s the thing — Toyama gave off major _Spider-Man game_ vibes. You know that slightly eerie feeling when the city’s empty and it feels like you’re the only one around? Yep. That. Kinda cool. Kinda spooky. 😅

After checking in, we quickly caught a train to **Takaoka**, home to one of Japan’s **Three Great Buddhas**. The weather was sunny but refreshingly cold — perfect walking weather. From the station, it was a peaceful **20-minute walk** to reach the statue.

While this Buddha wasn’t as towering as the one in Nara, it looked absolutely majestic with a **bright blue sky** behind him. The whole area was calm and quiet — almost meditative. Nearby, we stumbled upon a charming little **green tea shop** run by an elderly couple. They served us a warm sample, and it was so good we each bought a pack to take home. Simple joys. 🍵

From there, we made our way toward **Amaharashi Coast**, a scenic stretch of the stunning **Toyama Bay**. On the walk to **Etchū-Nakagawa Station**, we passed tiny bridges and gentle streams, including a **gorgeous red bridge** nestled in lush greenery. It was postcard-perfect.

![alt text](/images/japan/great-buddha.jpg)

And then came the moment that felt straight out of an **Instagram reel** — a tiny, adorable **orange train** emerged from the trees onto the tracks. With **blue skies, birds flying**, and crisp air around us, the whole scene just felt surreal.

![alt text](/images/japan/orange-train.jpg)

The ride to the coast was the highlight — **train tracks running almost at the edge of the sea**, waves crashing close by, and the sense that you’re floating just above the water. It was one of the most beautiful train rides we’ve ever taken.

**Amaharashi Coast** didn’t disappoint either. We clicked dozens of photos — on the tracks, at the crossings, with the ocean in the backdrop. But as the **cold wind picked up**, we found ourselves shivering and had to rush back to the station for shelter. ❄️

![alt text](/images/japan/amarahashi-coast.jpg)

We caught a **local bus** from there, looped back through **Takaoka Station**, and finally returned to Toyama. By nightfall, the once-empty streets had come alive a bit — lit windows, light traffic, and the quiet hum of a small city getting ready to wind down.

We stayed only **one night in Toyama**, and this was our final one. It was a cold, calm, and visually rich day — filled with scenic walks, quiet moments, and unforgettable ocean views. 🌌

### Day 6: Gorge Runs, Wrist-Wave Lockers & A Cold Night beside the Alps 🏞️🚴🍔

We checked out of **Hotel Livemax Toyama** after a short but sweet stay. Yesterday was all about **Toyama Bay’s western coast** — today we set off to explore the **eastern side**, with a big highlight on the way: the iconic **Kurobe Gorge**. Our end destination after sundown? The mountain city of **Matsumoto**, in **Nagano Prefecture**.

But first — **Kurobe Gorge**.

This place is famous for its jaw-dropping natural beauty — deep ravines, turquoise rivers, and misty mountains. The **Kurobe Gorge Railway**, originally built in the 1920s for dam construction, was later transformed into a scenic line that hugs the valley walls. It’s become a must-do for nature lovers and railway nerds alike.

We headed to **Toyama Station**, boarded a train to **Kurobe-Unazuki Onsen Station**, and were immediately greeted by a visual treat — a viewing window made of blue-tinted glass. The morning sun lit up the entire landscape, and through that dreamy glass, we took a perfect **silhouette photo with our luggage** — a moment we instantly decided to save for the final post of our Japan trip.

Now, here’s the thing. You _do not_ want to explore with heavy luggage — especially ones that have gained a few kilos thanks to some retail therapy. But Japan’s got us covered: **station lockers**. Most major train stations have them (still, check ahead to be sure). And guess what? No keys! Just **tap your Suica card**, and you’re good to go.

Even cooler? You can transfer your physical Suica to your **iPhone**… or in my case, my **Apple Watch**. Swiping my wrist to open a locker made me feel like I was in a sci-fi flick. It’s those little Japan moments that make your first international trip from India feel ✨next-level✨.

Outside the station, we grabbed a quick coffee at **7-Eleven**, sat at a little table with four chairs, and just soaked in the sunshine. Something about drinking hot coffee in a cold, quiet town hits different.

![alt text](/images/japan/shin-kurobe.jpg)

Now, to get to the gorge, we had to make a short walk from Kurobe-Unazuki Onsen Station to **Shin-Kurobe Station**.

> Quick Japan trivia:

> **“Shin”** (新) means “New” in Japanese. So when you see places like **Shin-Osaka**, **Shin-Kobe**, or **Shin-Kurobe,** it means a new station build for better access.

> Also,

> ShinKansen = Shin + Kansen = New + main line/ trunk line

From Shin-Kurobe, we continued to **Unazuki Onsen Station**, the gateway to the gorge. Surrounded by lush green hills, the vibe instantly shifted — it felt like we’d arrived in a hidden hill town.

We boarded the **iconic orange train** that looks like something out of an amusement park ride. Tiny, open-air, and low to the ground — but trust me, this thing was sturdy. It carried us along narrow tracks that snaked through **valleys, rivers, and dams**. The train curves were photogenic, and the **bridges over deep ravines** were breathtaking. We even had a stop where we could get down, pose with props, and take photos with postcard-perfect backdrops.

Oh, and remember — **Japan is earthquake-prone**, so we noticed active construction and safety measures even deep in the gorge. On the way back, **every single worker and railway staff** waved at our train in perfect sync. It was such a warm, beautiful gesture — it genuinely moved us. 🥹

**Always keep your camera ready** — in Japan, _beauty doesn’t announce itself — it just shows up._

After the scenic ride, we returned to the station and had a comforting bowl of **Tensoba** (tempura soba noodles) with the mountains smiling at us in the sun.

![alt text](/images/japan/kurobe-gorge.jpg)

Time to move again! We picked up our luggage from the locker — _one cool wrist wave_, locker opens — _bam!_ magic. Then, it was off to **Nagano Station**, and from there, a connecting train to **Matsumoto**. We reached around **7:30 PM**, and the cold hit us instantly.

Dragging our trolleys for a **20-minute walk** through chilly, quiet streets felt like a callback to our first day in Kyoto.

Our stay, **Ryokan Matsukaze**, sat near a split in the **Ta River**, right at the edge of the interfluve. Finding it at night, in the cold, was tricky. And when I say cold — **I mean cold**. Matsumoto is surrounded by mountains and feels like a frozen valley after dark.

When we finally arrived, we were greeted by the sweetest elderly couple, probably in their 70s. Communication was a bit of a struggle, but their warmth said everything. The best part? They offered **free bicycles** for us to use. 🚲

The room? Full-blown Japanese aesthetic — **futons on the floor, wooden interiors, paper windows**, and a set of cozy **yukata robes**. We changed, took some fun samurai-style photos, and just soaked in the calm of it all.

But hunger doesn’t care about vibes — so at around **10:30 PM**, we hopped on the bikes and cycled off in search of food. It was **9°C**, and we were **roaming the empty streets with jackets over**. Classic.

We ended up at **McDonald’s near Matsumoto Station**, warmed up with burgers and fries… and then had an unexpected adventure. One of our friend’s **bike chains came off**, and we spent the next **40 minutes in the cold** trying to fix it with freezing fingers.

Then, a heartwarming moment — a kind old Japanese man pulled into his driveway nearby, saw us struggling, brought out his **toolkit**, and tried to help us. Language barrier? Yes. Helpfulness? 100%. Even though it looked impossible, with one strong yank on the pedal, **the chain snapped back into place**. We laughed it off and rode back like heroes who just survived a side quest. 😄

![alt text](/images/japan/matsumoto.jpg)

Back at the ryokan, bellies full, hearts warm, toes frozen — we crashed into our futons and had the **best sleep**, surrounded by rivers, mountains, and a whole lot of memories.

### Halfway Through Japan: Reflections, Geography & Travel Wisdom 🗾✍️

So, we’ve reached the midpoint of our 14-day Japan journey — and _what a ride it’s been!_ From Kyoto’s quiet temple mornings to Toyama’s coastal charm, from deer-filled Nara parks to bullet train thrills and peaceful valleys of Kurobe Gorge… it already feels like we’ve lived a dozen little adventures.

#### A Quick Geography Crash Course (India-style Analogy) 🌍📍

Japan is made up of **47 prefectures** — these are kind of like Indian **states** or **districts**, but with their own local governance and unique culture. Each prefecture is part of a broader **region** (think North India, South India style). Here’s how our trip has moved so far:

*   **Tokyo & Narita** (Start Point) — Part of **Kantō region**, eastern Japan. Urban, cold in October, slightly humid.
*   **Kyoto, Nara, Osaka, Himeji** — These fall under **Kansai region**, more central-west Japan. Kyoto was traditional and calm, Osaka was neon and bustling, and Himeji felt like a living history book.
*   **Toyama, Takaoka, Amaharashi** — This is **Chubu region**, located on the **northern coast** facing the Sea of Japan. It’s chillier here, especially in the mornings and evenings. Toyama felt quieter, more scenic, and less crowded.
*   **Nagano & Matsumoto** — Inland and _mountain-wrapped_, part of the **Japanese Alps**. It’s cold here even in October — temperatures dropped to **9°C at night**, and we were cycling around in jackets and yukata robes like brave (and slightly frozen) samurai 😅.

In short:

*   **South & West** = Cultural and warmer
*   **North & Inland** = Scenic, colder, and nature-rich
*   **East** = Fast-paced and modern cities

Every new prefecture brought a fresh vibe, and traveling across them gave us a better sense of Japan’s incredible diversity.

> We were punching stamps in our stamp books in everyplace. Our first duty in any destination is to search for the stamp pad or stamp machine and get the seal in our stamp books

#### Don’t Let Overplanning Steal the Joy 🧭

You might’ve noticed — I’m _not_ going into exact train names, platform numbers, or bus routes. And that’s intentional.

> _“Too much information makes us rigid. Travel should have a plan — but never forget to leave space for wonder.”_

Japan’s public transport is incredibly reliable and easy to navigate with **Google Maps** and **Klook**. Type in your destination, and it’ll guide you like magic — down to the exact platform and even which carriage to board.

And if you ever feel lost? Trust me, **getting lost in Japan is half the adventure**. You’ll stumble upon a quiet street, a vending machine alley, or a grandma-run café with a handwritten menu. Those are the real moments.

#### Lockers: The Unsung Heroes of Travel 🎒🔐

Another life-saver we discovered: **locker services at stations**. If you’re hopping between places or making detours before reaching your hotel, you can simply store your luggage in coin-operated lockers (many accept IC cards like Suica/Pasmo). No dragging heavy trolleys through temples or tight streets!

Some lockers are even smart — just swipe your **Apple Watch** or **phone**, and they pop open like magic. It’s convenience meets sci-fi.

#### Travel Lessons So Far: 📝

*   **Pack light.** Your back and legs will thank you.
*   **Use Google Maps for trains.** It works like a charm.
*   **Always carry a backup power bank.** You don’t want your phone dying before clicking the Great Buddha.
*   **Stay flexible.** Leave time in your schedule to get lost, slow down, or just sit with a coffee and stare at the mountains.
*   **Start early.** Japan winds down early, and sunsets hit around 4:30–5:00 PM in autumn.
*   **Smile and bow.** Even if you don’t speak Japanese, respect travels fluently.

### Day 7: Into the Japanese Alps — Kamikōchi Wonders & a Castle Under Stars 🏞️🚴‍♂️🏯

We started the day fresh, dressed up, and finally got to see **Matsumoto in daylight** — and man, what a view. The rivers beside our ryokan were glistening, and the mountains in the distance wrapped the whole city like a protective blanket. It felt like waking up in the middle of a scenic desktop wallpaper.

We hopped on our **bicycles**, grabbed a quick breakfast, and parked them beside the **bus stand**, all set to begin our journey to **Kamikōchi**. But, as with travel, not everything goes as planned — we missed the bus we wanted. So we improvised, pedaled to **Matsumoto Station**, and caught a train to **Shinshimashima Station**.

**Shinshimashima** is a cute, tiny station — literally the last stop for trains. As you step out, the exit opens up into a bus bay that seamlessly connects travelers deeper into the Japanese Alps. We boarded a bus heading to **Taishō Ike Pond**, our first stop.

**Taishō Pond** was stunning — mirror-like waters, soft mountain reflections, and greenery all around. We went full tourist mode: **DSLRs out, phones out, fingers frozen from all the clicking.** 😄

From there, we took an hour-long walk through lush green trails running alongside the **Azusa River**. It was calm, surreal, and photo-worthy every 10 steps. Eventually, we arrived at **Kamikōchi**.

> _Kamikōchi is a 15-kilometer-long plateau nestled in the_ **_Japanese Alps_**_, sitting 1500 meters above sea level._

The place is a **natural masterpiece** — a crystal-clear river, swaying forests, wooden hanging bridges, and snow-dusted peaks far away. It probably looks its most magical in **full autumn** or **snow season**, but even now, it was jaw-dropping.

We grabbed a few bites — **Nikuman** (a fluffy steamed pork bun) and some classic **Japanese ice cream**, all while soaking in the mountain air. Though it had a decent crowd, it somehow stayed peacefully quiet — the kind of place that makes you whisper without even realizing.

After a satisfying walk and a heart full of views, we headed to the return bus stop. Then came the surprise of the day: **a cab turned out to be cheaper than public transport** for our group of four.

We flagged one and, for the first time in Japan, hopped into a **taxi**.

The cab driver? Super sweet. He had this **pocket translator device** — he’d speak Japanese into it, and it would spit out perfectly translated Tamil to us. Yep, **Tamil**. He didn’t need to, but once he learned our mother tongue, he switched to it out of respect. For the next **2 hours**, it felt like we were in a multilingual podcast — with Japanese on one side and robotic Tamil responses on the other. Weird? Yes. Hilarious and memorable? Absolutely. 😂

We stopped at a **7-Eleven** to withdraw cash (as most cabs accept **cash only**) and finally made it back to **Matsumoto** around 6 PM — it was already dark.

We hopped back on our cycles and headed to **Matsumoto-jō Castle** — and _wow_.

Lit up against the night sky, **Matsumoto Castle** looked like a floating fortress. The castle is surrounded by a moat, and the shimmering reflections made the whole scene feel ethereal. Cycling around in the dark, castle lights glowing, river on one side — it honestly gave off **Stranger Things vibes**, only missing the synth BGM. 🎵🚲

We wrapped up the day with a cozy dinner, chatted about how wild and beautiful the day was, and tucked ourselves in for a well-deserved night’s sleep — **our last night in Matsumoto**, surrounded by rivers, mountains, and the quiet kind of peace only a mountain town can offer.

### Day 8: Zenkoji Temple, Rain, Reflection & the Key to Paradise – A Calm Before the Climb 🌧️🍎🛕

We woke up to a soft drizzle and overcast skies. **Matsumoto**, once again, looked like a painting — misty rivers, silent streets, and mountains in the distance peeking through the haze.

This was hands down our **most relaxing day** of the trip so far — and honestly, we needed it.

We started the morning by catching a train to **Mash Café & Bed NAGANO**, a charming little place we’d be calling home for the night. The journey took about **1.5 hours**, and once we reached, we quickly checked in and dropped off our bags. After settling in, we took a bus from nearby bus stand. Took us 20 minutes to reach the destination for the day — **Zenkoji Temple**.

As we reached the temple area, we walked through a **grand torii gate**, and the drizzle started picking up. We ducked into a nearby shop for cover — and what luck! The baker inside was gently sugar-coating fresh **apple pies**. We grabbed one, added a scoop of ice cream, and sat down to enjoy.

**Rain, apple pie, and ice cream** — possibly the most unexpected but soul-satisfying combo ever. 🍎🍦☔

Then, we stepped into the grounds of **Zenkoji Temple**, one of the most spiritually significant temples in all of Japan. It’s said that Buddhism was introduced to Japan **over 1,500 years ago**, and the **first-ever Buddha statue** brought into the country from India, via the Korean Peninsula is enshrined here. That’s some serious ancient energy.

Despite being located in central Nagano, Zenkoji somehow felt untouched by time. It even holds the distinction of being **one of the few temple structures to survive WWII bombings**.

While exploring, we stumbled upon a small **ceremony** being performed — calm chanting, the scent of incense, and light raindrops hitting the old stone paths. It felt like time slowed down.

Then came the highlight: **Kaidan Meguri**, the pitch-dark underground passage.

You walk through this tunnel, completely blind, with just your hands feeling the walls around you. Somewhere in the middle, there’s a **“Key to Paradise”** — a metal latch you’re meant to touch for good fortune.

Even if you’re not claustrophobic, the darkness is so absolute, it’ll test your senses. Your pupils go from full daylight to _survival mode_. It’s strangely peaceful… but also eerie. A literal moment of “blind faith.”

After emerging from the dark, we sat down near the temple entrance, quietly gazing out at the rain-slicked streets and tiled rooftops. The air was cool, the wind gentle, and everything just… still.

We took our time heading back to our ryokan, grabbed a warm dinner, and called it an early night.

> _Yes, this day was slow. But it was_ **_intentionally slow_**_._

> _We needed to rest our legs, recharge our souls, and get ready —_

> _Because tomorrow? We tackle the mighty_ **_Japanese Alps_**_._

### Day 9: Into the Clouds – Hakuba Ropeways, Alpine Treks & Kung Fu Pond Vibes 🏔️🚡🥶

We woke up, packed our essentials, and hopped on a train from **Nagano to Hakuba** — a charming alpine village nestled in the **Japanese Alps**, just outside the city. Hakuba shot to fame as one of the host sites for the **1998 Winter Olympics**, and while it’s best known for its snow-covered magic during winter, we were here to see what its **green season** had to offer.

Spoiler: still stunning.

After arriving, we grabbed a **quick breakfast and hot coffee at Lawson**. There’s something about sipping a hot drink with **mountains towering around you** — even a simple convenience store feels cinematic.

We took a **20-minute walk** through the crisp mountain air and reached the **first ropeway station**. This one was an enclosed cabin-style car that fits about four people. We got in, and every little **jerk or sway** gave us a tiny adrenaline rush. The higher we climbed, the thicker the **clouds** became. At one point, we were literally gliding through them.

After some pictures in that misty heaven, we hopped on to the **second rope car** — a more open, bench-style lift that fits two or three people. We split up as two and two, and as we ascended even higher, **everything turned white**. Below us: lush green grass. Ahead: pure fog. The mist was so thick it left **droplets on our hair strands**, and the wind added to the chill.

As we approached the final ropeway station, a massive **T-shaped structure slowly emerged out of the fog** — honestly, it felt like a mission in the _IGI game_, like we were about to land into enemy territory 😆.

We got off at **Kurobishi Daira**, standing at a whopping **1680 meters above sea level**. And you guessed it — **photo and slow-mo video session** began. We couldn’t resist.

After soaking in the views, it was time for some action — our **trek to Happo Pond**. We started around **12:30 PM** and reached the pond by **1:15 PM**. And let me tell you, this place looked straight out of _Kung Fu Panda_ — like where **Shifu would take Po to find inner peace**. All it needed was a kung fu master meditating by the water. 🧘‍♂️

The **35mm DSLR lens** did wonders capturing the soft light, fog layers, and reflections. We trekked in pairs — two of us first, and the other two got a bit… let’s say, _creatively lost_. 😅

That meant more time for us to click different angles, wait for light shifts, and just breathe in the silence.

After regrouping, we began the **descent back to Kurobishi Daira**. As sad as it was to leave that incredible view, we were greeted by a silver lining — **Pilar Café**, perched in the mist like something from a fairytale. Naturally, you’d expect hot drinks in freezing weather, right?

Well, **we got ice cream**. And it was _so creamy and cold_, we ended up standing there in silence, just slowly eating, shivering but smiling.

Finally, we boarded the rope cars back down — open lift first, then the closed cabin. With the cold winds picking up, every stop felt colder than the last.

We reached the base, took a **bus back to Nagano**, and _crashed hard_ at our stay. Our reward? A **good old 7-Eleven dinner** — because after a trek like that, convenience store food tastes _gourmet_. 😋

And with that, we wrapped up our **last night in Nagano** — a city that gave us temple calm, alpine highs, and unforgettable fog-drenched views.

### Day 10: From Wands to Neon – Harry Potter, Tokyo Tower & the Borderless Future 🪄🗼✨

This was, without a doubt, **the most hectic day** of our entire trip. We had to leave **Nagano**, reach **Tokyo**, and squeeze in multiple iconic spots — all in a single day. But hey, who said magic comes easy?

We boarded an early morning train, traveling nearly **2.5 hours from Nagano to Tokyo**, and arrived by **9:30 AM**. First stop? The lockers at the station — because **Suica-card locker magic** is now part of our routine. Bags in, freedom unlocked.

From there, we dashed to catch another train to reach our first big experience of the day:

**The Making of Harry Potter — Warner Bros. Studio Tour Tokyo.**

We made it by **11:00 AM**, and from the moment we stepped in, we were **no longer muggles**. Wizards now. 🧙‍♂️

We tried on **Gryffindor robes** (and yes, we looked cool), then began the tour. It was full of surprises — entering the **Great Hall**, reliving **Quidditch matches**, walking through **Diagon Alley**, boarding the **Hogwarts Express**, standing at **Platform 9¾**, visiting **Hagrid’s Hut**, and wandering through the eerie **Forbidden Forest**.

They even had behind-the-scenes sections showing how **sound, music, and effects** were created for the films. Every fan moment came to life here — and it’s _huge_. Easily a **3–4 hour immersive experience**.

Back in the muggle world, hunger struck hard. We walked into an **Indian restaurant called Ganesha Dining** nearby, and the familiar **butter chicken and naan combo** hit home.

Fun fact: **Tokyo has a surprisingly large number of Indian restaurants and expats** — so good food isn’t hard to find.

As the sun began to set, we headed to the iconic **Tokyo Tower**, a former radio tower turned observation deck with game zones, cafes, and **a jaw-dropping top-deck view**. From up there, Tokyo’s skyline looked like a sea of glowing skyscrapers stretching endlessly. It honestly left us speechless. 🌆

And because it’s _our thing now_ — **we had ice cream at the top deck**. But this time, instead of mountains, we were surrounded by **city lights** and the hum of life below.

As we got back down, a **live band** was performing, and the streets were buzzing — go-karts zooming by, crowds cheering. **Tokyo Tower really is Japan’s answer to the Eiffel Tower**, just with more soul and less cliché. 😄

Next up: the **mind-bending world of TeamLab Borderless**. But first, coffee.

We stopped for a **freshly ground pour-over coffee** — beautifully brewed and just the jolt we needed after a long day.

Then came **Teamlab Borderless**.

There’s honestly no perfect way to describe it. With **mirrors, lights, projectors, and sound**, it feels like walking through a dream — or a sci-fi movie. At one point, it felt like we were in _Avatar_, and another, like floating through the **tesseract in Interstellar**.

Truly **an immersive, out-of-this-world experience**.

After our senses had been thoroughly blown away, we returned to **Tokyo Station**, collected our luggage with another trusty wrist tap, and finally headed to our stay in Tokyo.

That night, **we slept hard** — exhausted, amazed, and already missing the magic of the day.

### Day 11: Sleep In, Game Hard & Shop Smart in Tokyo 🕹️⌚🛍️

After all the running around and trekking over the last few days, our bodies finally hit snooze — **we woke up close to noon**, and honestly, we needed that rest.

Our first stop (again)? **Tokyo Tower**. But this time, no top-deck views or night lights — just **pure arcade fun**. We dove into **racing games, claw machines, VR setups, and even Squid Game-themed challenges**. The hours just melted away in this gamer’s paradise, and before we knew it, it was already evening.

Next up: **Akihabara**, Tokyo’s mecca for tech and anime lovers. We headed straight to **Yodobashi Akiba** for our mini shopping spree. Gadgets, souvenirs, and quirky finds — you name it, it’s here.

We didn’t stop there though. We made our way to **Bic Camera** and picked up some sleek **Casio watches** — because you can’t leave Japan without at least one! ⌚

This day was _definitely_ more relaxed than the others, and that was the best part. It gave us space to breathe, wander, and reflect on how far we’d come.

> **_Travel tip:_** _Don’t reserve_ one big day _just for shopping. Instead,_ **_buy things in bits and pieces_** _throughout your journey, especially when you’re near the shops._

> _That way, your final days won’t feel like a checklist frenzy — and you’ll still have room (and peace) to pick up small stuff like_ **_chocolates and souvenirs_** _without rushing._

We ended the day with a cozy dinner, made a few final purchases, and headed back to our **Tokyo den**, winding down slowly and soaking in the city one last time before the big wrap-up.

### Day 12: Bamboo Trails, Beach Rails & That Iconic Slam Dunk Crossing 🚉🌊🛍️

We woke up early and set off to **the southern side of Tokyo**, with a calm plan for the day: explore **Hōkokuji Temple** and finally visit the **famous Slam Dunk railroad crossing** by the coast.

Hōkokuji Temple is a peaceful little spot known for its **bamboo grove**. It doesn’t take long to explore, and apart from the swaying bamboo stalks, what caught our attention were some **seriously cool cars** cruising the quiet roads nearby. Very aesthetic, very Japan.

From there, we made our way to **Kamakurakōkō-Mae Station** — a name anime fans will instantly recognize. This is the **“Slam Dunk crossing”**, the exact railway crossing from the anime’s opening credits. And let me tell you — **this place is an Instagram magnet**.

Right beside the crossing is a road where **retro and luxury cars** glide by, and just beyond that, a short flight of stairs takes you straight down to the **beach**. The ocean breeze, smooth waves, and even **Sony water skaters** doing their thing made the whole scene feel alive.

We spent time soaking it in — capturing shots of the **train passing by**, recording slow-mo reels, and yes, joining the **huge crowd gathered not to cross the road, but just to film the crossing**. 😂

The vibe here reminded me of the old **Road Rash Pacific Coast map** — seriously, the resemblance was uncanny… just swap out the bikers for a peaceful train line.

After a bit of local shopping in the area, we headed back to Tokyo and made our way to **Ginza**, the high-end heart of the city.

Our mission? The **largest Uniqlo store in Japan**, and one of the biggest in the world.

Spanning **12 floors**, it had everything — **men’s, women’s, kids’ wear**, and more. We spent a good chunk of time shopping and winding down the day slowly.

> _Fun fact: if you’re visiting Japan,_ **_Uniqlo Ginza is a must-visit_**_, even just for the experience of seeing how organized, massive, and minimalist a fashion store can be._

Tomorrow would be our **final exploration day**, followed by a day purely reserved for shopping and airport prep. But what’s coming next?

Let’s just say — it was the **most enjoyable experience of the entire trip**, at least for me personally. A perfect send-off.

### Day 13: The Hakone Loop – From Zigzag Trains to Pirate Ships in the Mist 🏔️🚠🛳️

This was **my personal favorite day** of the entire trip — and trust me, it was _so_ worth the wait.

The day before, we picked up the **Hakone Loop Pass** at Tokyo Station. It’s a fantastic all-in-one ticket that lets you explore **the best of Hakone using different modes of transport** — trains, cable cars, ropeways, pirate ships (yes, pirate ships), and buses.

**🚆 Tokyo Station → Hakone-Yumoto Station**

We started our journey early, taking a 1.5-hour train ride to **Hakone-Yumoto Station**, the gateway to the mountains.

**🚞 Hakone-Yumoto → Gora (Hakone Tozan Railway)**

Next, we hopped on the **Hakone Tozan Railway**, a scenic mountain train that zigzags and switchbacks its way up steep slopes.

It was **drizzling and overcast**, the windows fogged slightly, and the misty greenery outside looked like something out of a Ghibli film.

⏱️ ~40 minutes of peaceful beauty.

**🚠 Gora → Sounzan (Cable Car)**

From Gora, we switched to the **Hakone Tozan Cable Car** — a steep ride that almost felt like going up a mountain in a moving staircase. It’s technically a train, but you can _feel_ the incline as it climbs straight up.

At **Sounzan Station**, we stepped out onto a massive **balcony overlooking a fog-drenched valley**, rain pouring and clouds drifting past us like a movie scene.

We took a bunch of **blue-tinted umbrella photos**, the soft glow of the hallway behind us contrasting beautifully with the cool, moody tones outside.

Pure cinematic gold.

**🚡 Sounzan → Togendai (Hakone Ropeway)**

We grabbed a quick hot coffee from a cafe and boarded the **Hakone Ropeway** — this was hands down one of the coolest parts.

As we floated above **Owakudani’s volcanic valley**, the clouds swirled below and around us. On clear days, you can see **Mount Fuji** — but honestly, the **fog made it even more mysterious**.

⏱️ ~30 minutes of floating through the sky.

**🛳️ Togendai → Hakone-Machi (Pirate Ship Cruise on Lake Ashi)**

Then came the _highlight_: the **Hakone Pirate Ship**. With cold winds hitting our faces and the rain still falling, we climbed to the **top deck** of the ship and braved the storm for **photos, videos, and raw emotion**.

It was honestly like stepping into **Pirates of the Caribbean**, only with misty Japanese mountains instead of Jack Sparrow. My DSLR got wet. I didn’t care. The view? **Mountains. Water. Fog. Ship. Wind.** Unreal.

As we neared the shore, the rain faded, the fog slowly cleared, and the **docking of the ship** — so gentle, so precise — was hypnotic to watch.

And then, the most unexpectedly beautiful moment of the day…

We stopped at a **7-Eleven right on the lake’s edge**, grabbed coffee, and stood by the water in the freezing drizzle, watching the pirate ship disappear back into the mist.

_Best 7-Eleven coffee of my life — not for the taste, but for the moment._

**🚌 Moto-Hakone → Hakone-Yumoto (Tozan Bus)**

With chilled hands and warm hearts, we boarded the **bus back to Hakone-Yumoto Station**.

⏱️ ~35 minutes.

**🚆 Return to Tokyo → Kabukichō**

We made our way back to **Tokyo**, and ended the day with a short stroll through the **infamous Kabukichō district**.

Just a casual walk, some light shopping, a quick dinner, and off to bed.

It was the **last full itinerary day** of our trip. And honestly?

This one alone was worth coming to Japan for.
